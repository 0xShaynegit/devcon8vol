Diwali in Mumbai

Devcon 8 lands in the run-up to the Festival of Lights. Here is what that means for your week.

At a glance

Diwali is the Festival of Lights, a five-day festival and one of the biggest dates in the Indian calendar. In 2026 the main night, Lakshmi Puja, falls on Sunday 8 November, two days after Devcon ends. Dhanteras, the opening day, is Friday 6 November, the last day of the event (see the shift planning table for the commute). Mumbai dresses up for it: buildings wrapped in lights, bazaars full of marigolds and sweets, and a lot of shopping.

Why it matters for volunteers

Expect busier roads, heavier shopping crowds and full hotels around the venue, especially in the last days of the event. Plan your commute with extra time.

The five days

The first three days are fixed. For the last two, check a local calendar closer to the time.

Diwali and the Devcon week

  • Devcon runs 3 to 6 November, and only Dhanteras (6 November) falls inside it. The main Diwali night is after the event.
  • The shopping rush builds in the days before Dhanteras, so the build-up reaches the city while you are on shift.
  • Travel, hotels and flights fill early around Diwali. If you have not booked, do it now.
  • If you are staying on after Devcon, 7 and 8 November are the best nights to see the city lit up.

Siddhivinayak and Mahalaxmi temples hold community pujas around the festival. If you go in, dress modestly and expect queues.

A free day out

Diwali is a public festival, so the best of it costs nothing. If you have one free day, this route covers the highlights.

TimeStopWhat to do
8:00 amDadar Flower MarketWatch vendors sell marigolds and roses for decorating homes. Loud, colourful and free to walk through.
11:30 amSouth Mumbai heritage walkTake the metro or a local train south. Walk past CSMT and the General Post Office to see the colonial buildings dressed up for the festival, then eat pav bhaji or vada pav at a street stall.
3:30 pmCrawford Market and BhuleshwarNarrow lanes selling clay lamps (diyas), coloured powders for rangoli and festive sweets.
6:00 pmMarine DriveGrab a spot on the sea wall as the sun sets and the streetlights come on. The city skyline glitters across the water.

In the markets, keep your wallet in a front pocket, stay alert and let the energy carry you. Mornings are calmer than afternoons.

Weather and packing

By early November the monsoon has cleared and Mumbai enters its dry season, which locals count among the best times of year to visit. These are typical November conditions, not a forecast.

Typical early NovemberWhat to expect
DaytimeHighs around 32 to 34°C. Warm in the afternoon sun.
Evening and nightAround 21 to 23°C, with a welcome breeze by the sea.
RainAlmost none, on average zero to one rainy day in the month.
HumidityModerate, around 60 to 70 percent.
  • Daytime: light, breathable cotton or linen in lighter colours, plus sunglasses and sunscreen.
  • Evening: day clothes are usually fine. Bring a light layer if you feel the cold, and for the air-conditioned venue.
  • Footwear: sturdy, comfortable closed-toe shoes that can take a bit of street dust.
  • No rain gear needed, so market walks and evening plans should be dry.

Fireworks, noise and air

  • Fireworks are part of the celebration, but Mumbai restricts them. In 2025 the Bombay High Court allowed bursting only for a short evening window, 8 pm to 10 pm on Diwali day, and only certified green crackers. The 2026 rules are not yet confirmed. Mumbai TBC
  • Even so, expect noise in the evenings around the main nights, so earplugs help light sleepers.
  • Smoke can cause short spikes in air pollution after dark. If you are sensitive, wear a light mask on evening walks.
  • Keep clear of anyone setting off crackers, and do not light any yourself.

Getting around

  • Metro Line 3 serves the venue at Bandra Kurla Complex. It is air-conditioned and avoids street traffic, so it is the safest way to reach a shift.
  • Taxis and ride-share cabs see surge pricing and long waits in the evening peak, roughly 5 pm to 9 pm, when traffic builds near the markets.
  • Local trains are the backbone of the city. They are very busy in the days before Diwali.
  • In South Mumbai, walking is often quicker than a car, and more atmospheric.
  • Keep some cash in rupee notes for street vendors and auto-rickshaws, even though cards and UPI work widely.

Sweets and food

Diwali is the season of sweets, and boxes of them are exchanged as gifts. Try kaju katli (cashew fudge), motichoor ladoo (tiny gram-flour balls pressed into a sweet), and the snacks sold at festival stalls. Savoury favourites include chakli, chivda and karanji, a Maharashtrian pastry filled with coconut and jaggery. For where to eat, see the food section of the Mumbai guide.

On a budget

  • The lights are free. Heritage buildings such as CSMT and the facades along Marine Drive are lit for the public, so a self-guided evening walk beats a paid tour.
  • Eat at street stalls and neighbourhood sweet shops. Seasonal sweets such as jalebi, rabri and fresh motichoor ladoo are cheap and everywhere.
  • For stays, look at guesthouses and hostels near the western suburban lines, such as Andheri West or Vile Parle, rather than the tourist hotels in Colaba or Bandra. Check the commute to the venue first, and ask in the Devcon Telegram group.
  • Travel by metro, local train and on foot. Cabs across the city in the evening are slow and expensive during Diwali week.
  • For local trains, travel in the off-peak morning hours to avoid the festive rush.

Small courtesies

  • "Happy Diwali" is the usual greeting, and a smile goes a long way.
  • Wear breathable cotton and sturdy walking shoes. Modest dress, with shoulders and knees covered, is respectful in local neighbourhoods and at temples, and comfortable in the humidity.
  • If you are invited into a home, take your shoes off at the door and bring something small, such as sweets.
  • Rangoli (floor art) at doorways and shop entrances is worth admiring, but step around it.
  • Some shops and services close or shorten hours around Lakshmi Puja, so do not leave errands until the last minute.
