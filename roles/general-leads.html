Role 10

General Leads

The overview of the whole event, and the people who respond when something goes wrong. For the lead role and anyone who wants to know how leads work.

The basics

General Leads have a broad view of the event and can support any area. They are also part of incident response: with so many people at a large event, things can happen, and the team has to be prepared for how to approach them.

Your team

Each area has two Volunteer Leads, one for the morning shift and one for the afternoon. Your Volunteer Lead is your first point of contact on shift. Names come from Volunteer Training Session #2 on 16 October 2026 and can change.

What every Volunteer Lead does

These duties apply to all Volunteer Leads, in every area.

  • Check in your team. Confirm that everyone assigned to your area has arrived.
  • Cover absences. If someone cannot make it, ask straight away whether standby volunteers are available. The standby team sits at the volunteer headquarters.
  • Assign breaks. In an area with ten booths, for example, arrange breaks of 15 to 20 minutes so everyone can walk, have a coffee, fruit or juice, and come back fresh.
  • Run the handover. See below.

Shift handover

Handover runs from 1 to 2pm, when the morning and afternoon teams are both at the spot. The afternoon team arrives from home or from earlier at the event, has lunch, then comes over.

  • Pass on context, examples, challenges, possible improvements and things to watch for.
  • Make sure any tools are understood, so the workstream lead, such as the production or ticketing lead, sees a smooth transition, especially for attendees.
  • You have up to an hour. Plan on at least 30 minutes on the first two days. By the third day there is less to pass on, so it can be shorter.
  • A proper handover lets the afternoon team keep the morning free, and the morning team the afternoon.
  • Afternoon leads also join a daily retro with the Devcon team.
